## Correction (Brian, 2026-11-27): that systematic term is board placement

The line above is wrong and is retracted. The board is put down by hand, so its
pose differs every session and there will always be a constant offset relative to
the stored `live/board.json`. The ≈ (−8, 0, +11) mm term is that session offset.
Baking it into `camera_in_tool` would convert a per-session placement into a
permanent camera error. `camera_in_tool` stays a hardware constant; the board
pose is registered per session.

Two checks on this dataset, both holding data already on disk:

```
stored board pose                         rms 38.31 px
board-only fit, z free                    rms 38.94 px
board-only fit, x y z free                rms 32.31 px
board-only fit, x y z yaw free            rms 31.09 px   (moved 15.7 mm, yaw +8.0 deg)
```

So a re-registered board recovers 38.3 → 31.1 px, with a 16 mm / 8° shift that is
exactly the size of a hand-placed mat. One board pose cannot do better than 31 px,
which is why the mount solve "improves" when you let it move too.

Joint 12-DoF fit (mount and board together, 23 views, `reports/wide3_jointfit_20261127.json`):

```
joint fit rms                      12.00 px
split fit (fit on half) 9.04 px  ->  held-out 17.43 px
fitted mount  dT 36.3 mm  dR 5.66 deg   (z -13.2 -> -49.4 mm)
fitted board  z 432.6 -> 399.8 mm
```

Held-out 17.4 px means the volume is still not internally consistent even with
12 free parameters, and the fitted mount moves 36 mm in z while the board moves
33 mm the other way. That pairing is the signature of a **distance-vs-focal-length
trade**, not of a camera that moved: at ~400 mm standoff a few percent of focal
length error trades almost one-for-one against range. The `fovy 47.0 deg` used
here was resolved against a B5 reference and is the term to question next, not
the mount.

**Conclusion for the product:** the per-session board registration path already
handles the term Brian named. Nothing here justifies editing `camera_in_tool`,
and the mount solve should not be used again as a calibration tool until the
focal/scale question is settled.
