## Per-view scatter (the reason) — 23 views

```
dT (view mount vs candidate)  median 17.3 mm   p90 29.5   max 31.8
per-view rms                  2.6 – 62.3 px    median 28.6
corr(board tilt, rms)        0.42
corr(markers available, rms) -0.47
```

Restricting to *only* the well-constrained views, which is the fair test:

```
thr>= 5  n=20  dT med 19.6  dR med 4.4°  -> delta [-8.9 -0.2 10.4] mm
thr>= 8  n=11  dT med 21.2  dR med 3.9°  -> delta [-8.9 -0.3 12.2] mm
thr>=10  n= 8  dT med 19.3  dR med 4.2°  -> delta [-7.1  2.8 10.9] mm
```

So weakness of individual views is **not** the explanation. Eight views each
carrying ≥10 markers still disagree with each other by ~19 mm and ~4° of rotation.
A camera bolted to the wrist cannot do that. The scatter is in the pose model.

What the threshold sweep *does* show is a stable systematic component:
every subset lands on ≈ (−8, 0, +11) mm from the current mount. That is a real
~14 mm mount error, consistent across views — worth taking eventually, but not
at a scatter of ±20 mm, and not without knowing which term is wrong.
