## 1. Occlusion model — the exact mismatch

Re-audited all 35 usable `_cal` frames with `ROBOT_GRIPPER=cad_parallel`:

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| mask coverage | **48.2 % on every frame** |
| decoded tags the mask suppresses | **32/62 = 52 %** (worst tag 99.9 % covered) |
| detector yield | 43 % |
| decoded-corner reprojection RMS | median **1.32 px** |

**The camera is right; the tool is not.** Reprojection at 1.32 px proves the
camera↔board relation, and the real frames prove the camera sees no tool — but
the model puts the installed tool across the right half of the view, and the sim
render agrees with the model. So the camera↔tool relation is wrong.

What I measured about the geometry: at a cal posture the tool's own vertices sit
**0–100 mm from the lens** (the generated tool pack mounts it millimetres from
the camera), and surfaces that cross the near plane rasterise into large
polygons. I tested whether that was a clipping artifact by raising the near
plane to a physical **20 mm** aperture (`near_plane_m` parameter, committed) —
**coverage stayed 48.2 %**, so it is real geometry in front of the lens, not a
sliver.

I cannot infer the correct mount from images that never contain the tool. A
photo of the wrist from the side (or the physical standoff between lens and
tool) would settle it. Until then the mask stays as it is, conservative, and
planning with it yields 0 poses.

The **2 % overlap rule** stays untouched: with the geometry wrong, any
false-reject rate measured against it is meaningless.

## 2. New finding — the REMOTE transition moves the arm

Measured with no motion command issued:

| event | shoulder_lift | elbow_flex |
|---|---|---|
| REMOTE, settled | 46.55 | −63.96 |
| → HOLD | 46.55 | −63.96 |
| → REMOTE | **49.71 (+3.16)** | **−63.34 (+0.62)** |
| → HOLD | 49.71 | −63.34 |
| → REMOTE | **52.79 (+3.08)** | −62.46 |
| → REMOTE again | **56.13 (+3.34)** | −61.85 |

- **Cumulative**: every HOLD→REMOTE adds ~3.1° of lift and ~0.6° of elbow.
  Over this session the arm went 33.4 → 56.1° of lift and the tip descended
  125.4 → **40.3 mm** above the board.
- **HOLD changes nothing**, and with no commands the arm is rock steady:
  **0.00° drift over 180 s** at 20 Hz feedback. So this is not sag; it is the
  transition.
- Suspect: `_open_follower()` runs `bus.connect()` → `write_calibration()` →
  `configure()` on every REMOTE, and that sequence re-homes or re-offsets the
  servos. Whatever the mechanism, **entering REMOTE must not move the arm.**
- Consequence: any joint-space plan is invalid a fraction of a second after
  arming. This also explains the 5a scan's early pose timeouts (2.2–2.3° at
  marker71/88, the first poses after REMOTE) far better than servo compliance.
- **Safety:** the tip is now 40 mm above the playing surface and each REMOTE
  lowers it ~3°. Do not cycle modes casually.

## 3. Settle fix — validated, and it works

- Manual elbow-only residual nudge: **1.57° → 0.34°**.
- `_hold_and_correct` driven against the real bridge with a one-pose plan:
  reached in 2.9 s, **final max error 0.70°** (elbow 0.64°, the rest of the max
  being the lift shift of the same transition). `pose_tolerance_deg` stayed
  **1.5**; `hold_s` 2.0; `correction_rounds` 2.
- Numbers: `reports/block5d_settle_controller.json`.

## 4. Also fixed — a second scan could never command

`ScanController._seq` started at 0 while the bridge rejects any sequence not
newer than the session-global `last_seq` (`seq 1 is not newer than
1795020098210`). `run()` now seeds `_seq` from the bridge state. Without this,
any scan after the first motion of a session fails on its first command.
