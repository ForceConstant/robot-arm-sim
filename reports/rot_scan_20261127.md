# Post-rotation sanity scan — 2026-11-27

Brian rotated the mat by roughly 10° and asked for the sanity scan. Question it
was built to answer: **does the constant 16 mm / 11.4° error belong to the board
or to the camera mount?**

**Answer: the board.** The discrepancy grew with the mat (+8.03° → +21.10° of
stored-vs-true board yaw, i.e. 13.07° of real rotation), instead of holding at
11.4° the way a mount error would. `camera_in_tool` stays untouched.

## What ran

Six poses on the real arm, illuminate-and-capture, gripper held at its measured
value (79.6°, not actuated), arm homed before and after. Intrinsics and mount
pinned at `handeye-2026-11-18T11-block4c`; the static board pose solved per view.

```
pose  markers   board x      y      z     yaw
C       10     -19.9    -28.8   435.5  -149.38
C+45     7     -25.4    -22.0   427.5  -155.32
C-45     9     -21.0    -28.9   425.0  -154.82
P2       6     -14.8     -5.4   423.3  -154.55
P3       6     -11.8    -23.5   436.1  -155.47
P4      10      -7.8    -25.3   437.7  -155.37
```

## Result

Pooled board pose: **t = (-17.4, -24.4, 431.5) mm, yaw -154.58°**, against a
stored `(x -27.6, y -31.6, z 432.6, yaw 184.32)`.

```
yaw shift from stored:  +21.10 deg now   vs   +8.03 deg before the rotation
                        -> the mat really did turn 13.07 deg
stored-vs-current rotation, per view:  21.6 deg median (5 of 6 views)
                        (the same quantity was 11.38 deg before the rotation)
```

The rotation discrepancy **tracks the mat**: it rose by roughly the amount the mat
turned. A mount rotation error is a property of the arm and would have stayed at
11.4° while the board turned underneath it. It did not.

This also closes the loop on the escalation: the "constant 16 mm / 11.4°" was the
stored board yaw being stale — exactly the per-session offset Brian described, and
why folding it into `camera_in_tool` would have been wrong.

## Two honest caveats

1. **One view is a PnP branch outlier.** Pose `C` solved 32.01° away from the mean
   of the other five (which agree to 5.2-7.3°). Ten markers were visible, so this
   is not a coverage problem: `cv2.solvePnP(..., SOLVEPNP_IPPE)` on a planar target
   has two valid solutions and the code takes the first. The earlier "16.24 mm /
   11.38° at C" baseline came from a single view of that same posture, so its
   precision was overstated. Fix: `solvePnPGeneric`, refine both candidates, keep
   the lower-residual one.
2. **These six views are a thinner basis than the 23-view volume** (rotation
   agreement 5.2-7.3° vs 1.64° across 23 views). Enough to answer board-vs-mount;
   not enough to be the new session registration.

## Consequence

`live/board.json` is now stale by ~21° of yaw and ~14 mm. Anything board-relative —
scan plans, square targeting, piece placement — needs a fresh registration for this
session before it is trustworthy. That is the designed per-session flow
(`chess_sim/board_registration.py`), and it is deliberately *not* a mount change.
