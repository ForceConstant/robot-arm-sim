## 3. The mismatch, precisely

The part's long axis is its **local z** (bounds 51.04 × 61.19 × **64.50 mm**).
A rotation of ±90° about **z** leaves that axis unchanged, so both fingers keep
pointing along z (down) — good — but the two bodies' **y separation collapses in
the camera view**: the two meshes end up along the same line, one behind the
other, which is exactly the single fused silhouette we see.

That says the two body positions are in a **different base frame** than the tool
model uses. The old bodies sat at y = −0.04146 and +0.02254; the new ones at
−0.03946 and +0.02054 — **2 mm less negative and 2 mm less positive**, i.e. a
4 mm narrowing, which alone cannot collapse a 60 mm gap. So the sign/order
mapping (which occurrence is which, and whether the assembly's +y is the tool's
−y) is not resolvable from the two numbers alone.

tool base frame *including its rotation order*, or a one-line confirmation of
which occurrence (+90 or −90) sits at the negative-y position. With that, the
swap is two numbers.
