Measured distortion (all frames, k3 fixed):
`[-0.116618, 0.802714, 0.002470, -0.000970, 0.0]`.

**Stability verdict — the all-frame lens is the usable one, and the cal subset
cannot determine focal length.** With the same constrained model the cal subset
lands 2.2 % away (813.8 vs 831.9), and every unconstrained variant on that
subset collapses to a different focal length (430, 128 px) at an *equal* RMS.
The 37 `_cal` frames are one board at essentially one standoff, so focal length
and range trade off; the 106-frame set spans u 8–550 px / v 8–476 px with a
median corner spread of 104 px, which is what pins the lens. First-order
estimates on the cal subset alone (same model) are 813.8, on the full set 831.9;
with the principal point held at the sensor centre they agree to 0.6 %
(842.6 vs 837.3). No stop condition was triggered: the subset does not
*disagree*, it is *under-determined*.

**Adopted lens (shared by the live file and the sim):**
`fx 831.897, fy 833.014, cx 320.0, cy 240.0`, distortion
`[-0.102251, 0.685733, 0.001112, 0.003239, 0.0]`, RMS **1.069 px**.

Why the principal point is forced to the centre, stated plainly: the
unconstrained solve puts it at (309.1, 242.3), but a MuJoCo camera only has
`fovy` — it cannot render an off-centre pinhole, so any off-centre value makes
"the sim and IRL views agree" unachievable by construction (~10.9 px in u).
The value is also not stable: (309.1, 242.3) on the full set, (293.0, 233.4) on
the cal subset, (305.5, 246.1) with k3 free — a ±16 px spread. Holding it at the
centre and refitting distortion costs **0.002 px** of RMS (1.067 → 1.069). The
comparison against the measured model is reported honestly in §5 (median
11.70 px), which is that principal-point term and nothing else.

## 2. Mount

`tools/block4c_mount.py`: one residual over every recorded marker corner of the
fixed-board `_cal` frames, mount `camera_in_tool` plus a flat board
(x, y, z, yaw) solved jointly, staged from a nominal-board bootstrap and then
polished with the measured distortion. FK uses the live chain, which reproduces
the scene (`chain_fk` vs MuJoCo camera: **0.0000 mm / 4.7e-7** at every cal
pose).

- views used for the mount: **18** (those with ≥2 markers; a single tag is a
  two-fold-ambiguous planar PnP). 23 cal frames carry a recorded detection, 24
  have any ChArUco detection, 14 of the 37 have none.
- reprojection over the 23 usable views, 256 corners: **median 1.527 px**,
  p90 2.71, RMS 2.599 (the RMS is inflated by the three clipped tags in §5).
- camera displacement from the stored mount: **14.28 mm**
- rotation difference from the stored mount: **178.69°**

That 178.69° is not a mount that moved: the stored matrix is an
OpenCV-convention solve whose camera y/z axes are flipped. Read as OpenGL —
which is what the file's schema, `WristCalibration.camera()`, and
`CameraCalibration.project()` all assume — it puts **all 264 board corners
behind the lens** (`tools/block4c_conv.py`). The re-solved matrix read as
OpenGL reprojects those corners at 1.71 px median. So the correction is
two-part: the lens was 2.66× wrong *and* the mount was written 180° rolled.

New mount (OpenGL camera axes, `so101:gripper` body frame — the file's
convention, no conversion applied):

```
[[-0.845366 -0.218268  0.487560  0.067656]
 [ 0.155227 -0.973702 -0.166759 -0.002916]
 [ 0.511136 -0.065290  0.857016 -0.010885]
 [ 0.        0.        0.        1.      ]]
```

Camera translation **(67.66, −2.92, −10.89) mm** in the tool frame. The "64 mm
off-axis" figure in the plan survives: the offset is real, it was the
orientation that was wrong. The stock CAD camera sits at (4.1, 44.8, −23.1) mm
and is 63 mm / 49° from this measured pose, which is exactly the mismatch that
made the sim predict markers where the bench saw none.

## 3. Board pose

Same `_cal` set, same lens, from the joint solve (flat board on the table):

| | x mm | y mm | z mm | yaw deg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| solved | **−9.92** | **−3.13** | **431.35** | **5.67** |
| `live/board.json` | −13.74 | −3.57 | 409.5 | 7.14 |
| delta | +3.82 | +0.44 | **+21.85** | −1.47 |

**The z shift is the lens.** The stored board z was produced by PnP under
`fx 312.77`; depth scales with focal length, so the same pixels implied a board
21.9 mm too close. At the measured lens the playing surface is **431.35 mm**,
which is **2.35 mm** from the configured/live `board_z_m` 433.7 and the sim
board top — the three now agree to a few millimetres. x/y/yaw move by
4 mm/0.4 mm/1.5°, inside the ±8 mm / ±2.5° placement envelope Block 3
validated.

A free 6-DoF board in the same fit finds a 0.5° tilt and z 430.35 mm — i.e. the
same board to 1 mm. The flat model is kept because the bench board is lying on
a mat and Block 3's named procedure fits a plane; the free fit is reported as a
cross-check, not adopted.

## 4. Sim camera

`chess_sim/scene.py` gained `CameraMountMeasured` and
`WRIST_CAMERA_MEASURED`; `_add_wrist_camera` uses it when the custom (printed)
gripper pack is active. Pose is written in the same OpenGL axes and the same
`so101:gripper` body frame as `camera_in_tool`, so **no convention conversion is
applied to the pose**. The one conversion that is needed is the lens: MuJoCo has
only `fovy`, so

```
fovy = 2·atan((H/2) / fy) = 2·atan(240 / 833.014) = 32.1445°
```

with square pixels (`fx = fy = 833.014`). The quaternion is written **w x y z**
(MuJoCo order) — `0.0974007 0.2604418 −0.0605141 0.9586559` — and the round-trip
back to a matrix is checked in `tools/block4c_camera.py`.

Verified: `chain_fk(q) @ camera_in_tool` vs MuJoCo `cam_xpos`/`cam_xmat` at the
cal poses is **0.0000 mm / 4.7e-7** (`tools/block4c_render.py`), and
`reports/block4c_sim_calibration.json` re-exports the scene with 0.000000 mm.
The standalone XML export `chess_sim/assets/scene/chess_so101.xml` was updated
to the same camera line and its visual proxy box keeps its 10 mm stand-off.

## 5. Camera view match (the acceptance test)

`tools/block4c_viewmatch.py`: for each cal view, pose the camera with the *sim*
rig (FK @ measured mount), project the ChArUco grid at the measured board pose,
and compare against the corners the real camera recorded — undistorted with the
measured lens so the comparison is ray-for-ray (the principal point cancels).

**Valid-view rule (reproducible in the tool, thresholds unchanged at 2 px median
/ 4 px max):** a view counts only if it has **≥2 detected markers** and **no tag
clipped by the frame border**. A tag is clipped when one of its corners is
closer to the image border than one marker module — the marker's own black
border is `edge_px / 6`, plus 1 px of slack. Single tags are excluded because a
four-corner planar PnP is two-fold ambiguous and gives no cross-check.
